# The 100% Async Cold-Start Strategy

> **How to launch GYFH, recruit partners, and build prestige without making a single phone or video call.**

---

## 🧩 The Chicken-and-Egg Dilemma: What Comes First?

The biggest trap first-time organizers fall into is asking: *"How can I ask judges to join if I don't have participants? And how can I get participants if I don't have judges?"*

Here is the secret: **Neither comes first. Structure comes first.**

```
[Phase 1: Structure]       1-page landing site + published 100-point rubric + 3 clear tracks.
                                   │
                                   ▼
[Phase 2: Local Anchor]    Ask 1-2 friendly teachers/mentors: "Can we list you as Faculty Advisor?"
                                   │
                                   ▼
[Phase 3: Industry Judges] Cold email fintech engineers: "2 hours async video review against published rubric."
                                   │  (They say YES because the ask is tiny and the site looks legitimate)
                                   ▼
[Phase 4: Prestige Badge]  Put judge titles & company logos on the website: "Judged by engineers from X, Y."
                                   │
                                   ▼
[Phase 5: Student Launch]  Now pitch students & high school CS clubs. It looks elite, organized, and prestigious.
```

## 🤫 Leading as an Introvert: The 100% Async Rulebook

You **never** need to jump on an awkward Zoom call or phone call to run this event. Many of the most respected developers and hackathon leads run everything entirely through text.

### 1. Recruiting Co-Organizers Without Calls
- **Never do video interviews.** A 20-minute Zoom call tells you if someone is charismatic, not if they can configure a bot or write clean emails.
- **The Async Audition**:
  1. Have them fill out a 3-question form (portfolio link, role interest, weekly hours).
  2. Send them a realistic task in Discord text or email:  
     - *If Marketing*: *"Draft a 3-sentence DM to an economics club president in New York."*
     - *If Tech*: *"Add Ticket Tool to our Discord test server and test an open ticket."*
  3. If they deliver high-quality work in 48 hours without needing hand-holding, they are hired. If they ghost or make excuses, you avoided a bad hire without wasting an hour on video.

### 2. Communicating with Judges Without Calls
- In your outreach email, **explicitly promise that judging is 100% asynchronous**:
  > *"The judging is completely asynchronous: between Sunday March 28 and Tuesday March 30, you simply review 4–5 short (3-minute) student demo videos in your own time using our Google Form scoring rubric."*
- Busy engineers **love** this. They hate scheduling 1-hour Zoom calls. Telling them it's async increases your response rate by 300%.

### 3. Weekly Team Coordination Without Calls
- Run an **Async Standup** every Monday on Discord (`#core-chat`):
  1. *What did you finish last week?*
  2. *What will you finish this week?*
  3. *What is blocking you?*

## ⚖️ Solo vs. Recruiting: What You Actually Need

Can you run GYFH by yourself?
- **You CAN easily do solo**:
  - The website (HTML/CSS/JS or Next.js/Vercel)
  - The Discord architecture and bot configurations
  - The judging rubric, tracks, and submission forms
  - Judge email outreach (batching 15 emails every Saturday morning)
- **Where you will suffer if you are 100% solo**:
  - Reaching out to 50+ high school clubs around the world (it gets tedious).
  - Answering participant questions 24/7 during the 48-hour event window.
- **The Ideal Lightweight Setup**:
  You don't need a large committee. You just need **ONE good Outreach Co-Lead** (or a team of 10–15 Student Ambassadors) whose only job is sharing the link with their clubs and schools. You handle all Tech & Operations.

## 🏛️ How to Manufacture "Prestige" (Honest Positioning)

High school students only care about participating if an event feels **selective, legit, and resume-worthy**. You don't create prestige with fake claims; you create it through **institutional signals**:

1. **A Published 100-Point Rubric**: 95% of student hackathons say "we will judge based on creativity and tech." Publishing a detailed 100-point rubric (Technical Execution 25pts, Financial Viability 25pts, UX 20pts, Compliance 15pts, Innovation 10pts, Pitch 5pts) immediately screams institutional caliber.
2. **"Student-Founded, Industry-Advised"**: Name your high school, but add advisor names.
3. **Specific, Serious Tracks**: Avoid generic "build whatever" prompts. Having tracks like *"Social Finance & Decentralized Savings Circles"* makes serious builders excited.
4. **Verified Credentials & Awards**: Offer verified digital certificates (e.g. via Accredible, Certifier, or signed GitHub badges) for all completed submissions, plus category awards like *"Best Compliance Architecture"* and *"Best Youth UX"*.
