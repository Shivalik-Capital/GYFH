# GYFH Competition Tracks & Plain-English Rubric

## 🧭 The 3 Problem-First Tracks (YC / Request for Startups Style)

We don't tell students what to build. We define the problem, and builders choose the medium (web app, mobile app, bot, extension, visualizer, or API).

---

### Track 1: Making Money Make Sense (Education & Literacy)
- **The Problem**: Most teenagers learn about money only after making their first expensive mistake. Traditional education is either boring textbook lectures or dubious advice from influencers.
- **What We Want to See**: Tools that make tricky concepts—like compound interest, inflation, debt traps, credit scores, or taxes—instantly intuitive and impossible to ignore.
- **Could Be**: An interactive visual simulator, a real-time inflation shock comparison tool, an AI money co-pilot, a game, or an educational browser extension.

---

### Track 2: Smarter Everyday Money (Saving & Budgeting)
- **The Problem**: Banking apps are built for adults with salaried 9-to-5 jobs, not students managing small allowances, gig earnings, or part-time wages. Meanwhile, every consumer app is engineered to drain your wallet.
- **What We Want to See**: Systems that help young people save, budget, and manage small amounts of money without thinking about it. Frictionless tools that make saving feel rewarding instead of painful.
- **Could Be**: Automated spare-change savings vaults, chore-to-allowance allocation apps, student spending guardrails, or micro-investing visualizers in developer sandboxes.

---

### Track 3: Social & Shared Finance (Community & Group Money)
- **The Problem**: Money is social, but today's financial tools are isolating. Splitting group expenses, collecting club dues, or pooling funds for a cause usually ends up in messy spreadsheets, awkward reminders, and lost funds.
- **What We Want to See**: Tools that let groups of people pool, manage, lend, or donate money together with 100% transparency. Safe, modern ways for communities to collaborate financially without speculation.
- **Could Be**: Group trip expense vaults, transparent student club treasuries, rotating savings circles (chit funds/tontines), or testnet verifiable badges for community work.

## ⚖️ Plain-English Scoring Rubric (100 Points Total)

| Weight | Question Asked | What Earns Top Scores |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **25%** | **Does it work?** | A working functional prototype. Can judges click buttons, enter data, and see it work? Clean code, real logic, and zero fake mockups. |
| **25%** | **Does it solve a real problem?** | Does this project genuinely help young people handle money better, or is it just a useless gimmick? |
| **20%** | **Is it easy to use?** | Can someone understand your product in under 60 seconds without reading an instruction manual? Clean, thoughtful design. |
| **15%** | **Is it safe and ethical?** | Protects student privacy, strictly uses fake sandbox money, and avoids manipulative tricks, hidden catches, or gambling mechanics. |
| **10%** | **Is it original?** | A creative, fresh approach rather than rebuilding a generic expense calculator that already exists everywhere. |
| **05%** | **3-Minute Demo Video** | A short, clear video showing who it's for, the problem it solves, and a live demonstration of your project in action. |

## 🏆 What Every Team Receives

1. **Official Verified Certificate**: Every team that submits a working project gets a signed digital certificate for college applications, LinkedIn, and resumes.
2. **Real Engineer Feedback**: Written evaluations and constructive feedback on code and architecture directly from fintech engineers.
3. **Software Credits & Featured Showcase**: Category winners receive free developer API tiers, software credits, and permanent portfolio spotlights on the GYFH website.
4. **Special Category Awards**: Dedicated awards for Best Beginner Team (first-time hackers), Best UX Design, and Best Safe/Ethical Product.
