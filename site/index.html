Request for Builders

The Three Problems

We don't tell you what to build. We give you the problem. You choose the solution—whether that's a web app, a mobile tool, a browser extension, a bot, or a visual simulator.

Problem 01 // Education Track 1

Making Money Make Sense

The Problem

Most teenagers learn about personal finance only after making their first expensive mistake. Traditional financial education is either boring textbook lectures or dubious advice from social media influencers.

What We Want to See

Tools that make tricky concepts—like compound interest, inflation, debt traps, credit scores, or taxes—instantly intuitive. We want things people actually enjoy using instead of feeling lectured to.

Could be: An interactive visual simulator, a real-time inflation comparison tool, an AI money co-pilot, a game, or an educational browser extension.
Problem 02 // Daily Life Track 2

Smarter Everyday Money & Saving

The Problem

Modern banking apps are built for adults with salaried 9-to-5 jobs, not students managing small allowances, gig earnings, or part-time wages. Meanwhile, every consumer app is engineered to drain your wallet.

What We Want to See

Clever systems that help young people save, budget, and manage small amounts of money without thinking about it. Frictionless tools that make saving feel rewarding instead of painful.

Could be: Automated spare-change savings vaults, chore-to-allowance allocation apps, student spending guardrails, or micro-investing visualizers in developer sandboxes.
Problem 03 // Community Track 3

Social & Shared Finance

The Problem

Money is social, but today's financial tools are isolating. Splitting group expenses, collecting club dues, or pooling funds for a cause usually ends up in messy spreadsheets, awkward reminders, and lost funds.

What We Want to See

Tools that let groups of people pool, manage, lend, or donate money together with 100% transparency. Safe, modern ways for communities to collaborate financially without speculation.

Could be: Group trip expense vaults, transparent student club treasuries, rotating savings circles (chit funds/tontines), or testnet verifiable badges for community work.
Scoring Standards

How You Are Judged

No subjective popularity contests. Every project is scored out of 100 points by 3 independent industry judges using these plain criteria:

25%
Does it work?
A working functional prototype. Can judges click buttons, enter data, and see it work? Clean code, real logic, and no fake mockups pretending to be functional.
25%
Does it solve a real problem?
Does your project genuinely help young people handle money better, or is it just a useless gimmick?
20%
Is it easy to use?
Can someone understand your product in under 60 seconds without reading an instruction manual? Clean, thoughtful design that respects the user.
15%
Is it safe and ethical?
Protects student privacy, strictly uses fake sandbox money, and avoids manipulative tricks, hidden catches, or gambling mechanics.
10%
Is it original?
A creative, fresh approach rather than rebuilding a generic expense calculator that already exists everywhere.
05%
3-Minute Demo Video
A short, clear video showing who it's for, the problem it solves, and a live demonstration of your project in action.
Minor Protection

Zero Real Money & Supervised Mentorship

GYFH is 100% educational. No real bank credentials or live funds are permitted. All project work takes place in test sandboxes (like Plaid sandbox, Stripe testmode, or Sepolia testnets). Mentors only communicate in supervised public Discord channels.
